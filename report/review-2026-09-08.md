## Scope

This review covers `report/impactor-confidence-report.md`, `report/impact-comparative-analysis.md`, `data/impactors/*.json`, `data/comparisons/*.json`, `data/comparisons/notes.md`, and the scripts, as committed at 0c39d1a. Claims were checked against the repo's own data files and against a small number of remote sources: MPEC 2026-R64, MPEC 2026-J143, the ESA NEOCC past-impactors table and its 2026 JN4 page, the CNEOS fireball API, and the JPL InSight press release on the December 2021 Mars impact. No broad search was done. Items marked "verify" rest on my recollection and were not confirmed remotely.

No files other than this one were changed.

## Overall

The ranking report is tight and mostly holds up against its own data and the MPECs. The comparative analysis is where the problems concentrate: several factual errors, a register that drifts into commentary, and a policy-history section that appears three times across the repo. Nothing in the ranking itself changes, but roughly a dozen sentences need correcting before either document is quoted.

## Factual corrections

Ordered by how much they matter.

1. **Velocity comparison mixes two quantities.** The analysis ("What the 13 tell us about the objects") contrasts 2026 RW1 at "~5 km/s" with "12.8–21 km/s for everything else." The 5 km/s is the encounter velocity before Earth's gravity acts; the others are atmospheric entry speeds from CNEOS. RW1's entry speed is about 12.3 km/s, and 2008 TC3's encounter speed works out near 6 km/s. RW1 is the slowest, but not "a clear outlier" and not meaningfully "gentler" than TC3. The dossier field `velocity_km_s: 5` in `2026-rw1.json` is inconsistent with the other twelve dossiers for the same reason. The ranking report's own wording ("at a slow 12 km/s" and "lowest relative velocity") is correct.

2. **Aphelion count is wrong.** Ranking report, "Orbits are ordinary NEO orbits": "Ten of 13 have aphelia inside 3 AU" should be nine. 2024 UQ sits at 3.74 AU, between the two groups.

3. **Warning-time-by-aperture claims contradict each other and the table.** The ranking report ("The population is a selection effect") credits "the 1.5 m Mount Lemmon telescope and Pan-STARRS" with 7 to 21 hours, but Pan-STARRS discovered none of the 13, and Mount Lemmon gave 3.6 h for 2022 WJ1. It also gives Piszkéstető "2 to 3 hours" when its three cases span 2.0 to 6.7 h. The analysis substitutes Bok for Pan-STARRS, so the two documents disagree with each other.

4. **L'Aigle is about 110 km from Saint-Pierre-le-Viger.** "Almost to the location" in the analysis table, and "a little over a kilometer" in the `2023-cx1.json` narrative, are both wrong. Same region of Normandy is the defensible statement.

5. **Jupiter is about 318 Earth masses**, not "forty times Earth's mass" (analysis, Synthesis).

6. **"Every other body's impact record is reconstructed after the fact" is contradicted by the document's own tables.** SL9 was predicted 16 months out, and WE0913A's lunar impact was predicted weeks out. The claim only survives with "at meter scale" made load-bearing, and even then the Chang'e booster is a meter-scale predicted impact on another body.

7. **2020 SO did not pass "inside the geostationary belt."** Perigee was about 50,000 km from Earth's center; the belt is at 42,164 km. Confirmed against the Wikipedia article the dossier cites.

8. **InSight's December 2021 impactor was not "comparable to Chelyabinsk (hundreds of kt)."** A 5 to 12 m body carries tens of kilotons at most, and JPL's release says it would have burned up in Earth's atmosphere. Appears in the analysis table, the prose below it, and `planetary-impacts.json`. The Daubar et al. 2020 title as given in the JSON ("Two Chelyabinsk-sized asteroid impacts on Mars...") also looks wrong; verify.

9. **Tunguska "10–15 Mt (modern reanalysis)" misstates the reanalysis cited.** Boslough and Crawford 2008, listed in `earth-undetected.json`, argue for 3 to 5 Mt. Give the 3 to 15 Mt range and replace "roughly 2,000 times larger" with "hundreds to thousands of times."

10. **H chondrites do not "dominate" witnessed falls.** L chondrites are at least as common among falls, and the set contains an L. The defensible signal is two achondrites in four falls against roughly 8 percent of falls generally. Both reports make the H claim.

11. **"Every meteorite case fell over populated land at night."** Almahata Sitta fell in unpopulated desert and Motopi Pan in a game reserve. "Over land" is enough. Separately, `2018-la.json` and `2024-rw1.json` call the entries "daytime," but both were after dark locally, at 18:44 CAT and 00:39 PHT.

12. **"The same sensor gap northwest of Australia."** The Arafura Sea is north of Australia, about 2,000 km from RW1's impact point, and JN4's 4,500 km corridor means its impact point is not known. ESA's JN4 page says the Darwin witness narrowed the corridor to north of the Northern Territory. The claim appears in both reports and in `2026-rw1.json`.

13. **"Ratio ranges from 0.2 to 10" for modeled versus measured energy.** The repo holds three pairs: 2008 TC3 near 1, 2024 RW1 near 1, 2022 EB5 near 10. No 0.2 exists in the data.

14. **2026 JN4 impact time.** Calling ESA's 15:39 "a table error" is a hypothesis. ESA's past-impactors page (updated 2026-07-29) lists JN4 with 15:39:28 UTC, 17.34 km/s, 0.0156 kt, and 0.5 to 1.2 m, and counts it as its twelfth impactor while the MPC declines to list it. State the discrepancy and the catalog difference; leave the diagnosis out.

15. **Darwin eyewitness sourcing is backwards.** `2026-jn4.json` says the witness was "not independently verified" and cites the repo's own report by absolute local path. ESA's JN4 page is the source; cite it and drop the self-citation.

16. **Smaller dossier errors.**
    - `2024-xa1.json`: "76 obs used in fit" is 2024 RW1's number; SBDB gives 60 for XA1. "Within about two hours (07:31 to 07:50)" is 19 minutes. Orbit class is "APO" where the others say "Apollo." Impact time 16:14:53 versus 16:15:01 in `rank.py`.
    - `2024-uq.json`: says 21 km/s "tied 2024 RW1," whose own dossier says 19.7.
    - `2022-eb5.json`: "Piszkeoto" and no diacritics on the discoverer's name.
    - `2026-jn4.json`: impact time 13:44 where `rank.py` uses 13:36.

17. **V00 is a Catalina Sky Survey telescope.** The Bok discovery of 2024 XA1 belongs to CSS, so CSS has seven of 13, not six. The analysis also claims "at least four" G96 staff observers when three are named and the 2026 RW1 observer is unidentified (the MPEC names none).

18. **MPEC 2026-R64 lists 29 observations**, with per-station counts that differ from the MPC Explorer's 30: G96 5 versus 3, V00 5 versus 4, E10 5 versus 9, F52 2 in both. Say which count the table uses. The MPEC's elements (a 0.945, e 0.183, i 1.43) also differ slightly from the SBDB values in the dossier.

19. **MPEC 2026-J143 gives different elements for 2026 JN4** (a 1.33, e 0.34, i 10.9) than SBDB (1.49, 0.43, 13.0). A six-observation orbit is solution-dependent; the statement "all inclinations below 13 degrees" depends on which solution is used.

20. **MPC does not compute impact probabilities.** The `2023-cx1.json` narrative says "the Minor Planet Center's automated systems flagged it as an imminent impactor." That was Scout, Meerkat, or NEOScan.

21. **Benešov fragment count.** I recall Spurný et al. 2014 reporting four stones, not three. The A&A page returned 403; verify before publishing.

22. **`scripts/fireballs.sh` cannot run as committed.** The `for d in ...; do` line is missing, leaving a bare `done`.

## Overreach and tone

- **"The pace accelerated after, and largely because of, external events."** The paragraph itself concedes the data cannot separate institutions from technology. Delete "largely because of" and keep the timing observation.
- **"Nothing close to Chelyabinsk scale has ever been caught in advance" as evidence of a "gap in kind."** A Chelyabinsk-class event occurs about once in several decades, so its absence from an 18-year window is expected from the flux alone. The sunward blind spot is real and can be stated on its own; it should not be inferred from this absence.
- **Solar radiation pressure "pushed back out" 2020 SO and J002E3.** Temporary captures escape by three-body dynamics; radiation pressure was the diagnostic, not the driver. The cited Wikipedia article does not support the escape attribution.
- **"We catch a few percent."** The denominator is events above 0.1 kt, but most recent pre-impact discoveries were below 0.1 kt. Different populations. The CNEOS database shows 21 to 38 events per year at or above 0.1 kt over 2008 to 2025, not 30 to 40. On the other hand, the database's smallest entry ever is 0.048 kt, which makes the RW1 sensor-gap argument stronger than the report currently states. As of 2026-09-08 the database still has no entry for 2026-09-06; its latest entry is 2026-08-15.
- **"Nine of the 13 were discovered between September and March."** With March included the count is ten, and the window covers seven of twelve months, so the expectation is about 7.6. There is no signal here.
- **U as "best-determined orbit."** U is a coarse logarithmic parameter describing future longitude drift. Tying three objects at U=4 does not make a 30-observation orbit equal to an 883-observation one. The relevant quantity is impact-point uncertainty.
- **"Effectively unity" for 2026 JN4's impact probability.** Six observations, U=9, and a 4,500 km corridor. Report the Scout or Meerkat probability if available; otherwise say "high."
- **In-flight counts include post-impact observations.** For 2019 MO three of seven observations and for 2024 UQ three of nine were precoveries found after the event. The asterisk covers warning time only; say so or exclude them from Obs and Tel.
- **Garbled sentence.** "Differentiated, more fragile material may be more likely to survive detection" does not parse; fragile material is less likely to survive.
- **DART "lent political support."** Unsourced in `notes.md`, and the AIDA concept predates Chelyabinsk.
- **Commentary to remove or compress.** Section (c) of `notes.md` is self-described as unverified generalization. The "general pattern" paragraph in the analysis repeats it. The heading "Humanity's reaction" and phrases such as "civilization-ending," "practice run," "vivid, undeniable events," "Old World telescopes could compete with the large American surveys" (`2022-eb5.json`), and the Synthesis's "overclaims/undersells" framing are the enthusiastic register to minimize. Two factual paragraphs on the 1998 goal and the 2013 institutions are enough.
- **Register mismatch.** The ranking report has zero em-dashes and no italic emphasis; the analysis has 55 em-dashes and leans on italics and bold lead-ins. Match the first document's voice.

## Overlaps

- The analysis's "What the 13 tell us about the objects" restates the ranking report's "What the class tells us" with different numbers. Give each observation one home and cross-reference.
- The SL9 and Chelyabinsk policy history appears in `notes.md`, in the analysis, and again inside the significance fields of `planetary-impacts.json` and `earth-undetected.json`.
- The "same sensor gap" claim appears in both reports and the RW1 dossier.

## What is missing

In rough priority.

1. **Alert time, not discovery time.** When did Scout, Meerkat, or NEOScan first flag each object, and at what probability? 2024 UQ's 1.8 h warning was zero usable warning. This column would separate "predicted" from "linked" cleanly.
2. **Corridor length at last observation** as the in-flight confidence metric, replacing or supplementing U. The XA1 dossier already records this trajectory from 400 km to 220 m.
3. **A precovery and latency table.** Five of 13 were in someone's data before the credited discovery: 2019 MO (F52), 2024 UQ (703), 2024 XA1 (both I41 and I52 preceded V00), 2026 JN4 (I41), and 2026 RW1 (F52). Tabulate by how long, and whether the link was made before impact. This is now the recurring theme and it is scattered across prose.
4. **Discovery circumstances to substantiate the selection-effect claims.** Distance, elongation, rate, and magnitude at discovery are asserted in prose but most dossier magnitude fields are null. H is in `sbdb_orbits.txt` and never surfaces in a table.
5. **A pre-impact physical characterization column.** Spectrum for 2008 TC3, rotation for 2023 CX1, colors for 2022 WJ1. The report treats this as an aside; it is the scientific payoff of the exercise.
6. **A flux baseline by size bin** so "a few percent" becomes a number with a stated denominator.
7. **The catalog discrepancy stated once, plainly.** MPC lists 11, ESA lists 12, the repo counts 13. ESA had not yet posted 2026 RW1 as of its 2026-07-29 update.
8. **Deliberate and predicted impacts on other bodies** as the honest contrast set, or an explicit exclusion sentence: SL9, LCROSS, DART, the Apollo S-IVB lunar impacts recorded by ALSEP.
9. **One sentence on the sunward hemisphere.** All 13 were night-side ground discoveries; no space-based asset has contributed a discovery. That is the factual form of the Chelyabinsk point.
10. **Ranking sensitivity.** Equal weighting of four ranks is a choice; show how the order shifts without U. The "Evid. rank" column has no documented rubric and refers to "the earlier ordering," which is not in the repo.
